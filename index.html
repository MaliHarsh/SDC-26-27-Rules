PODIUM · CY2027 Team Guide

ASME Student Design Competition · Stage 1 of the Engineering Games · Rules v1.0 (Aug 2026)

The 60-second version

PODIUM is a track-meet style contest. One robot competes in five Disciplines; your All-Around Score is the sum of your best score in each (max 700). Every run is 2:30: 30 s autonomous, then 2 min driven. No practice runs, one run per Discipline.

Building a legal robot

Size. Must fit a 40×40×40 cm cube on its own at every run start and at inspection. After the start signal it may expand to at most 75 cm horizontally and 120 cm tall. Bring your own rigid container (cardboard or fabric not allowed unless the Head Judge approves at that event).

Weight and power

  • 15 kg max including batteries and stored air. Console, tether, tools, laptop don't count.
  • Electric only. No internal combustion, fuel or combustion of any kind.
  • Any battery ≤ 24 V nominal; control system ≤ 24 V. Commercially made, undamaged, secured, with an easy-to-reach main switch or breaker. Any chemistry allowed.
  • Store and charge in a fire-safe charging bag.

Stored energy, pneumatics, hydraulics

  • Springs and elastics are allowed. You may pre-load, but the robot itself must be able to fully restore that energy during the run (e.g. re-wind a winch).
  • Pneumatics: 60 psi max anywhere, a visible gauge and a relief valve are required.
  • Hydraulics (if used): under 100 psi, non-flammable food-grade fluid.

Control

  • One control link only: a tether or one wireless link on license-free bands (2.4 / 5 GHz).
  • Tether must stay completely slack and can't be tied to anything or anyone. Tension draws a Minor Penalty; wireless is encouraged.
  • Teams must be able to disable the robot on request. Your laptop can't control or assist the robot during a run.

Required and prohibited

Required
  • Team Identifier visible
  • Flat 3×3 cm area for a Pair marker
  • Ascent anchor point near the top, rated for full robot weight
  • Gauge + relief valve if pneumatic
Prohibited
  • Flight or thrust lift (tethered or not)
  • Sharp edges, shattering materials, hazardous chemicals
  • Lasers above Class 1, pyrotechnics
  • Unsafe launches or objects leaving the Field
  • Anything that damages Field, objects or robots

Safety and inspection

Safety beats performance. Officials can stop any robot, run or activity at any time. The Head Judge has final authority.
  • Eye protection: bring and wear your own ANSI Z87.1-2020 / EN166 rated eyewear whenever robots are worked on or run. ASME provides none.
  • Batteries: fire-safe bag for storage and charging; remove and report damaged or swollen batteries immediately.
  • Stop fast: main switch or control link must stop the robot quickly; the Crew must disable it when asked.
  • Powering: at the team table you may power the robot for repair but not drive it. Drive only on a Field during your run.

    How a run goes

    • Called to a Discipline: 60 seconds to place the robot in the Starting Zone. Not ready? The run starts anyway. Not present at all = disqualified from that run (no grace period).
    • Robot comes out of its container and sits in the Starting Zone on its own, then the Crew steps back. One person presses one start control.
    • Autonomous (30 s): nobody touches the robot or console, no inputs. Breaking this voids the autonomous bonus.
    • Crewed (2 min): up to 4 Crew operate via the Operator Console. Only Crew can talk to Officials at the Field.
    • After the run the Scoring Official shows your score; confirm before leaving. Once confirmed or once you leave, it's final. No video replay.
    • Check the Field from the viewing area before that Discipline's first run; after that it's accepted as-is.

    Eligibility quick facts

    • Undergrad engineering students who are current ASME Student Members; one team per student; one robot per team.
    • A Team Captain (ASME member) and at least one faculty or professional mentor of record. Mentors advise but the robot must be student work.
    • Rule questions go through the official Q&A form; answers are published every two weeks.

    The five Disciplines

    The Sprint · 100 pts

    Slalom through 4 Gates (60 cm openings, in order, alternating sides) and over 2 Hurdles (5 cm tall) in a 2×7 m lane as fast as possible. Clock stops when the robot fully crosses the finish; no finish = 0.

    Score = 100 − 1 per second of finish time − penalties + auto bonus. Range 0–100.
    • Gate missed −15 · Hurdle gone around −10 · Reset −15
    • +10 if the robot passes Gate 1 on its own in the autonomous phase

    The Lift · 120 pts

    Carry Dumbbells (Light 1 kg, Standard 2 kg, Heavy 4 kg, ~15–20 cm across) from the loading zone onto a 3-tier podium (20 / 40 / 60 cm, multipliers ×1 / ×2 / ×3). Scores if resting on a tier and supported by the podium at the final buzzer.

    DumbbellTier 1Tier 2Tier 3
    Light 1 kg51015
    Standard 2 kg102030
    Heavy 4 kg204060
    • Autonomous placements ×1.5. Max 120.
    • Stacking allowed; the stack counts at the tier of its bottom weight, but if any weight would fall without the robot, the whole stack scores nothing.

    The Cycle · 140 pts playoff basis

    Collect soft 65 mm balls and pass them through goals. Low Goal: 40 cm floor-level opening. High Goal: 30 cm opening centred at 1.0 m. Ball supply ~1.8 m from the goals. A ball counts when it passes fully through.

    Score = (Low × 2) + (High × 5), autonomous balls doubled. Max 140.
    • Control at most 3 balls at once; herding or pushing more draws a Minor Penalty.

    The Marksman · 160 pts

    Land 8 Bean Bags (13 cm, 150–200 g) on a 60 cm ringed floor Target whose center is 2.5 m beyond the No-Cross line. Too far to reach, so launch, toss or slide. The robot starts holding at most one bag; a Loader (one Crew member) hands bags over one at a time in the Crewed Phase.

    Score = sum of ring values (bullseye out: 20, 16, 12, 8, 4; more than half inside the ring). Autonomous bag doubled. Max 160.
    • Floor-touching parts stay behind the line; an arm or launcher may reach over it in the air.
    • Autonomous: the robot may launch its starting bag and fetch more from the loading station, with no human help.
    • Special rule: launchers must start each run unloaded. No pre-tensioned launchers here.

    The Ascent · 180 pts most points

    Climb a vertical wall (≥ 2.2 m) with rungs, holds and bars. Height is measured floor to the lowest point of the robot. Only the highest Band held stably at the final buzzer counts; touching the floor at the buzzer = 0. The robot carries nothing up.

    Band 390135
    Summit120180
    • An Official clips a slack safety line to your anchor point before each run. It may not help you climb.

    Scoring, rankings and the playoff

    All-Around Score = best Discipline Score in each of the five Disciplines, up to 700. It ranks Day 1 and seeds the playoff. Maximums are applied after all bonuses.

    Tiebreakers

    1. Higher best-Discipline score, then second-best, and so on
    2. Faster best finish time in the Sprint
    3. Coin toss by the Head Judge

    Day 2 playoff

    • Top-ranked teams (by All-Around) each invite one partner to form Pairs (typically 4–8). All Pairs are formed before any match. A declined invite stays available for others.
    • Seeded single-elimination; top seeds get byes if the count isn't a power of two.
    • Every match = The Cycle + one Featured Discipline. Default: semifinals Sprint, final Ascent, third-place Marksman.
    • Match score = sum of both partners' runs in both Disciplines (four scores). At big events each Pair may run just one robot per Discipline. Higher total advances.
    • No defense of any kind, in any match.
    Design takeaway. Your partner is chosen for complementary strengths, and the Cycle is always played in the playoff, so a solid Cycle robot is valuable for getting picked.

    Design report rules

    • Optional but required for any judged award (Laurel, Blueprint, Ingenuity, Spirit, Rising Star).
    • Due 30 days before the event's first day through the official online form. Late = not accepted.
    • Single PDF, 10–30 pages (cover, references and appendix don't count). Pages past 30 aren't read.
    • Doesn't affect any performance score or the playoff.

    Rulebook cross-check

    check "Mech-E can be pre-stored." True, but the robot must be able to restore that energy itself (Rule 7.3-3). In the Marksman, launchers must start unloaded, so pre-tensioned launchers are banned there.
    check "Launcher can cross the line." Only in the air. Anything touching the floor must stay behind the No-Cross line.
    check "Working PSI of 60." 60 psi is the legal maximum. Design a margin below it, and add the visible gauge and relief valve.
    check "Pick up to 4 kg of dumbbells." A Heavy dumbbell is 4 kg, and the robot plus load must still be ≤ 15 kg, so budget weight carefully.
    Extra things the rules suggest
    • The Ascent is worth the most (180) and autonomous climbing is worth ×1.5, so an early climb design pays off.
    • The tether must stay slack, so wireless is the safer choice.
    • Team logo: it's the Team Identifier, and you also need the 3×3 cm flat spot for the Pair marker.
    • Report deadline is 30 days before the event; start the notebook now.
